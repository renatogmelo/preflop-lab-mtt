# Phase 6.7 Unified vs Decomposed Comparison

## Unified convergence at 10,000 iterations

| Game | Ground-truth value | Exploitability | NashConv | Strategy L2 to one reference equilibrium |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Sequential Hidden Choice | 0.666667 | 0.001382 | 0.002763 | 0.003496 |
| Public Signal Game | 0.555556 | 0.001510 | 0.003019 | 0.004252 |
| Coupled Decision Game | 0.000000 | 0.000010 | 0.000020 | 0.335619 |

The Coupled Decision Game has multiple equilibrium representations: exploitability and NashConv approach zero while distance to the particular enumerated equilibrium remains non-zero. Strategy distance alone is therefore not an equilibrium certificate.

Across budgets `100, 500, 1,000, 2,500, 5,000, 10,000`, unified exploitability fell monotonically in all three games. Sequential fell from `0.016933` to `0.001382`, Public Signal from `0.013840` to `0.001510`, and Coupled from `0.000422` to `0.000010`.

Anderson improved the finite-budget result in every game, but none of the decomposed variants reached a small raw residual. A reported ratio of zero in two final steps reflects an unchanged mapped response on that observed step; it is not a contraction proof.

## Initialization dependence

The damped decomposition ended at different strategy hashes from uniform, first-action-biased and second-action-biased starts. The Coupled game was most consequential: final exploitability was approximately `0.347777`, `0.347777` and `0.124830`, while normalized residuals remained near `0.940918`. This is path dependence without fixed-point success.

## Diagnosis

- Insufficient inner solving is present as a finite-budget error source, but is not enough to explain residuals near one after 250 deterministic inner iterations per subsolve.
- Frozen continuation strategies make the initial re-solve react to beliefs generated by the previous initial strategy.
- Updating the initial strategy changes conditional continuation reach and can cross response boundaries.
- Observed response ratios above one show non-contractive directions; damping shrinks the applied step but not the raw equation error.
- Anderson reduces some errors but cannot turn a non-small residual into a solved fixed point.

The unified solver avoids this external consistency problem by optimizing all information sets in one tree. This does not prove that every unified architecture will scale, but it answers the small-game research question positively.
